Support for staying on schedule

Multiple prescriptions and different dosing times can be difficult to remember. When medication reminders are included in an approved service plan, a caregiver can provide non-medical prompts at the appropriate time and report concerns according to agency procedures.

Reminder support may include

  • Verbal reminders to take medication according to the prescribed schedule
  • Encouragement to follow established routines
  • Reminders that a prescription may be running low so the client or family can take action
  • Observation and reporting of missed reminders or concerns according to the care plan
  • Coordination with other approved personal assistance routines
Medication reminders are not medication administration. Primetime caregivers do not prescribe medication, change dosages, make clinical decisions or independently administer medications.

Who may benefit?

Reminder support can be useful for eligible seniors, people with disabilities, clients with memory challenges, or anyone whose authorized plan includes assistance staying consistent with daily routines.

Frequently asked questions

Will a caregiver decide when medication should be taken?

No. The caregiver follows the schedule established by the client’s healthcare provider and authorized care plan.

Can a caregiver change a dose?

No. Changes to medication or dosage must come from an authorized healthcare professional.

Can reminder support be combined with personal care?

Yes, when both services are allowed and included in the client’s authorized plan.

How often are reminders available?

Scheduling depends on the client’s authorization and the approved service schedule.
